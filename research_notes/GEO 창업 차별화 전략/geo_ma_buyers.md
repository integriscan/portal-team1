# GEO/AEO M&A and Acquirer Demand (2024 – Oct 30, 2026)

Method note: Primary documents were opened directly where possible. These were SEC EDGAR filings (HubSpot 10-K FY2025 and 10-Q Q2 2026, Adobe 10-Q Q3 FY2026, Semrush 10-K FY2025 and DEFM14A, Yext 10-Q Q2 FY2027, Similarweb and Wix 20-F, Zeta 10-Q Q2 2026) and company press releases (HubSpot, Adobe, Zeta, Searchable/PR Newswire). The trade press opened was Calcalist, B&T, TechTarget, ZDNet Korea and Donga via Nate. Value Add VC and a guptadeepak.com M&A roundup were also opened. Facts marked **[snippet]** come only from search-result summaries; I did not open the underlying page. WebFetch was blocked for many domains in this sandbox, so pages were pulled with curl or Playwright instead.

## 1. Deal list: every GEO/AEO/AI-search-marketing acquisition found

### Takeaway
I verified seven pure GEO/AEO deals in about 12 months (Oct 2025 – Oct 2026). They sit in four clear price tiers:
- About $1M: Yext–GoShine (3 people)
- About $2M: Semrush–TopicRanker (asset deal)
- "Seven figures": Searchable–Meridian
- About $30M headline: HubSpot–XFunnel (about 10 people). The filed figure is $17.9M purchase price plus $12.2M of retention pay.
- About $225M reported: Sitecore–Scrunch
- $1.87B filed: Adobe–Semrush
- Undisclosed: Zeta–Senso

The headline XFunnel "$30M cash" is accurate only as a total. HubSpot's 10-K shows that about 40% of it is employment-contingent compensation, not purchase price.

### Inferences
- **Observed price bands:**
  - **About $1–2M** (Yext–GoShine, Semrush–TopicRanker, Searchable–Meridian at seven figures): 1–3 people, bootstrapped or pre-seed, under $1M ARR, under 12 months old. Effectively a paid acqui-hire or product tuck-in.
  - **About $15–30M total** (XFunnel; HubSpot's matching Dashworks and Futurepedia pattern of about $17–19M cash plus $7–12M retention): about 10–25 people, seed-funded, credible product used by enterprise customers.
  - **About $225M** (Scrunch): 2–3 years old, about $26M raised, 500+ brands, enterprise logos.
  - **$1.87B** (Semrush): public, $460M+ ARR, about 4x ARR, including $38M AI-product ARR.
- Buyers in the small bands pay for **team and time-to-market, not IP**. XFunnel's technology was valued at only $1.45M on a replacement-cost basis, against $16.7M of goodwill and $12.2M of 3-year retention. GoShine's tech was valued at $0.6M.
- In XFunnel, Senso and GoShine, the **target was already a vendor, customer relationship or partner** of the acquirer before the deal. Commercial integration preceded M&A.
- Even the scaled asset drew thin competition: in the Semrush auction, 8 of 10 contacted buyers declined NDAs. Buyer demand is concentrated in a few strategics rather than broad.

### Gaps
- XFunnel's exact seed amount and headcount at close. PitchBook's "10 employees" is snippet-only, and HubSpot does not disclose these.
- The Scrunch price is unconfirmed by any filing (Sitecore is private). Earnout structure ("up to") is unknown.
- Senso and Meridian prices, headcounts and founding years were not disclosed.
- No GEO deal by agency holding companies or PE roll-ups was found.

It measures brand mention rank and share across ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity and feeds SNS/PR consulting. - For a Korean GEO startup, domestic strategic exits are currently unproven. Large Korean agencies have shown a build preference: Cheil and Plaid shipped their own tools within months. A Korean exit would more plausibly come through (a) a global martech buyer seeking Korean/Naver coverage, (b) a tuck-in acqui-hire by a domestic digital agency later, once build fatigue sets in, or (c) a startup-to-startup merger like Searchable–Meridian.

## 3. Buyer-side strategy statements (build vs. buy)

### Takeaway
Two patterns hold. Strategics that already own **adjacent data** (Wix, Squarespace, Cloudflare, Similarweb, Korean agencies) **build**. Platforms that need **speed or measurement depth** (HubSpot, Adobe, Sitecore, Yext, Zeta) **buy**. HubSpot is the clearest evidence that a bought GEO tool can be monetized fast.

### Cited Findings
- **HubSpot, Q1 2026 call:** "AEO is a big opportunity, and we are leaning hard into it. Organic traffic for our customers is down 27% this year… AEO is an effective, nascent, but fast-growing [lead source]." Over 15,000 Pro+ customers activated AEO trials, and 28-day free trials were introduced. — [Motley Fool transcript Q1 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/05/07/hubspot-hubs-q1-2026-earnings-transcript/)
- **HubSpot, Q2 2026 call:** "We launched HubSpot AEO in April, both within Marketing Hub and as a stand-alone product… 32% of Marketing Hub Pro+ customers have activated AEO and nearly 16,000 customers have activated a stand-alone AEO trial in Q2." Trials "will slow deal cycles." — [Motley Fool transcript Q2 2026](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/08/12/hubspot-hubs-q2-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)
- HubSpot's 10-K now lists "answer engine optimization ('AEO')" as a core part of its system of engagement. — [HubSpot 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1404655/000119312526046646/hubs-20251231.htm)
- HubSpot AEO went GA Apr 14, 2026, about 4.5 months after close. Reviewers call it "essentially XFunnel rebuilt inside the HubSpot interface" (analysis blog). — [guptadeepak.com](https://guptadeepak.com/who-is-buying-building-ai-visibility/)
- Standalone price $50/month, also included in Marketing Hub Pro/Enterprise **[snippet]**. — [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/enterprise-software/news/366641773/HubSpot-builds-answer-engine-optimization-into-its-platform)
- **Adobe** (Anil Chakravarthy): "marketers who aren't optimizing for that world today will find themselves invisible tomorrow." Adobe cites AI traffic to US retail sites up 269% YoY (March 2026). — [Adobe press release](https://news.adobe.com/news/2026/04/adobe-completes-semrush-acquisition)
- Adobe's 10-Q says Semrush "enhances our Adobe Experience Manager offerings with search engine optimization and generative engine optimization solutions." — [Adobe 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/796343/000079634326000156/adbe-20260828.htm)
- **Semrush, pre-deal, built its own:** the 10-K says "Brand discoverability is increasingly determined by both traditional search algorithms and conversational answers from large language models," and describes its AI Optimization (AIO) solution. — [Semrush 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1831840/000162828026013259/semr-20251231.htm)
- **Sitecore** (CEO Eric Stine): "the internet must be written for machines to understand if we want humans to experience it." — [B&T](https://www.bandt.com.au/sitecore-acquires-scrunch-in-potential-225-million-deal/)
- **Zeta** (CEO David Steinberg): advantage lies in "infrastructure that connects an enterprise's data and context to those models." Senso brings "AI engineering talent and deeper connectivity to the frontier models and personal agents." — [Zeta press release](https://www.zetaglobal.com/news/zeta-global-to-acquire-senso-adding-ai-engineering-and-frontier-model-connectivity-to-its-intelligent-ai-infrastructure/)
- **Similarweb built:** its 20-F says Digital Marketing Intelligence "incorporates GenAI visibility solutions, which provides insights into brand presence and competitive positioning within AI-generated responses." It lists as a risk "changes in how information is discovered… by… answer engines… including shifts toward answer-engine optimization." — [Similarweb 20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1842731/000184273126000018/smwb-20251231.htm)
- **Wix built:** AI Visibility Overview launched July 2025 (analysis blog). — [guptadeepak.com](https://guptadeepak.com/who-is-buying-building-ai-visibility/)
  - Wix Ventures was a seed investor in XFunnel **[snippet, PitchBook]**.
  - Wix's 20-F FY2025 has no GEO acquisition; its big deal was Base44 (no-code). — [Wix 20-F](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1576789/000162828026015222/wix-20251231.htm)
- **Other builders and partners** (analysis blog, dated Sept 2026): Squarespace and Cloudflare built their own (Cloudflare's AEO Visibility Dashboard went to early access Aug 6, 2026). Optimizely built adjacent pieces and **partnered with Conductor**. Salesforce, Contentful, Sanity and Drupal had shipped nothing native. Salesforce Ventures invested in Bluefish. — [guptadeepak.com](https://guptadeepak.com/who-is-buying-building-ai-visibility/)
- No earnings-call GEO statements from WPP, Omnicom, Publicis or Dentsu were located in this pass.

### Inferences
- The most likely next buyers are those without adjacent data that are visibly exposed:
  - Salesforce (it holds a Bluefish stake via Salesforce Ventures)
  - CMS/DXP laggards (Contentful, Sanity, Acquia/Drupal, Webflow)
  - CRM and marketing-automation vendors following HubSpot's lead
  - Mid-cap martech consolidators like Yext and Zeta, which already do small tuck-ins
- HubSpot's disclosed adoption figures (32% Pro+ activation, about 16k standalone trials) show the buyer's payoff is **distribution × a fast-shipped feature**. A small GEO team is worth most to a platform with a large installed base that can bundle the tool immediately.

### Gaps
- Could not open official transcripts from Wix, Yext, Similarweb or agency holdcos to quote specific GEO statements. Q3 2026 HubSpot results were not yet out on Oct 30.
- HubSpot has not disclosed paid AEO customers or AEO revenue.

## 4. Investor activity and bubble/consolidation signals

### Takeaway
Disclosed GEO/AEO venture funding is about **$619M across 10 startups as of Oct 16, 2026**. The page appears to have been updated from the earlier "$604M" figure. Profound holds about 55% after a $180M Series D at $1.8B. Capital is concentrating in one leader, and the "undifferentiated middle" is being pushed toward exits.

All from [Value Add VC (Oct 16, 2026)](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/ai-search-optimization-startups-2026-geo-aeo-funding-investors) unless noted:
- About $619M disclosed across 10 startups as of Oct 16, 2026. The page's URL slug and older search snippets cite **$604M**, so the figure was revised upward, likely after Peec's Oct 13 $40M disclosure.
- **Profound:**
  - $180M Series D at **$1.8B** on Sept 15, 2026, co-led by Sequoia and Kleiner Perkins, about $335M total.
  - Revenue tripled in the 6 months before the round, and it reports 1,000+ enterprise customers (per TechCrunch).
  - Series C was $96M at $1B in Feb 2026 (Fortune).
- **Peec AI (Berlin):**
  - $21M Series A in Nov 2025 at more than $100M valuation, with ARR above $4M 10 months after launch.
  - By June 2026, more than $10M ARR and reportedly in talks at a **$200M pre-money** (TNW citing Sifted).
  - Disclosed $40M raised in total on Oct 13, 2026.
- **Others:**
  - Bluefish: $68M total; Series B of $43M in Apr 2026 (Threshold/NEA, with Salesforce Ventures and Amex Ventures).
  - AirOps: $62.5M total; $225M valuation in Nov 2025.

One claim says agencies with GEO service lines get a 1–2x multiple premium (citing FE International) **[snippet, vendor blogs]**. — [dealflow-os](https://dealflow-os.com/roll-up/seo-agency), [ctacquisitions](https://ctacquisitions.com/prepare-your-business-for-sale/seo-agency-exit/)

### Inferences
- The category shows classic early consolidation: one unicorn, a handful of well-funded challengers, and tiny tools exiting at $1–2M or merging with each other (Searchable–Meridian).
- GEO dashboards are becoming platform features (HubSpot, Wix, Squarespace, Cloudflare, Semrush/Adobe). That caps standalone valuations for monitoring-only tools while making them attractive tuck-ins.

## 5. Implications: can a small GEO startup be acquired in 2–3 years, by whom, at what price?

### Takeaway
Yes. Small-team exits are the **most common** GEO deal type so far. Most of them are acqui-hire or tuck-in sized (about $1M–$30M) and paid heavily in retention. A $100M+ outcome so far has required Scrunch-level scale: 2–3 years old, about $25M raised, 500+ brands, Fortune 500 logos.

### Cited Findings
- **XFunnel (about 10 months old, about 10 people per PitchBook [snippet], seed-funded) sold for about $30M headline:** $17.9M purchase price plus $12.2M of 3-year retention. HubSpot was a customer first, then an investor. The product was enterprise experimentation-focused ("enables enterprise brands to experiment and test quickly") and shipped inside HubSpot about 4.5 months after close. — [HubSpot 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1404655/000119312526046646/hubs-20251231.htm), [Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/h10u7lwg11x), [HubSpot newsroom](https://www.hubspot.com/company-news/hubspot-to-acquire-xfunnel)
- **GoShine (about 11 months old, 3 people, bootstrapped, tens of customers)** sold to Yext for $1.1M cash. — [Yext 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1614178/000162828026059784/yext-20260731.htm), [Calcalist sponsored](https://www.calcalist.co.il/article/s1chttndfl)
- **Meridian (about 11 months old)** sold to a peer for "seven figures"; its customers migrated. — [PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ai-visibility-platform-searchable-acquires-new-yorks-meridian-in-seven-figure-deal-302883134.html)
- **Senso** was acquired by a customer/partner (Zeta) whose GEO product it already powered. — [Zeta press release](https://www.zetaglobal.com/news/zeta-global-to-acquire-senso-adding-ai-engineering-and-frontier-model-connectivity-to-its-intelligent-ai-infrastructure/)
- **Scrunch (founded 2023, about $26M raised)** sold for about $225M reported, roughly 3 years after founding. — [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/searchcustomerexperience/news/366643973/Sitecore-acquires-Scrunch-for-answer-engine-optimization)

### Inferences
- **Realistic price bands for a small GEO team (2026 evidence):**
  - 1–3 people, under $0.5M ARR: about $1–3M, mostly as acqui-hire or retention.
  - 8–20 people with an enterprise-grade product and a design-partner relationship with the buyer: about $15–30M total. Expect about 40% of it as 3-year retention, which means equity value to investors is about 55–60% of the headline.
  - $10M+ ARR or 500+ brands with enterprise logos: $100M–$250M, as with Scrunch (and the reported Peec talks at $200M pre-money as a private-round reference).
- **What made XFunnel attractive at 10 months:**
  1. It was in HubSpot's own evaluation as a vendor first, so de-risked fit came before the investment and then the deal.
  2. Its experimentation and recommendation workflow ("monitor, experiment with, and strengthen") fit HubSpot's "Loop Marketing" narrative and AEO roadmap.
  3. A tiny, senior team could rebuild the product inside HubSpot within months. The buyer paid for the team: tech was valued at replacement cost of $1.45M.
  4. The buyer faced urgent defensive pressure: customer organic traffic was down 27%, and AI leads convert 3x.
  5. Network proximity: a Wix Ventures seed [snippet], and HubSpot's interest in Israeli AI teams.
- **Most likely acquirer types in 2–3 years:**
  - CRM and marketing-automation suites (HubSpot-style)
  - CMS/DXP vendors without adjacent data (Sitecore-style; next could be Contentful, Acquia, Webflow, or Salesforce)
  - Local/listings and martech consolidators doing small tuck-ins (Yext, Zeta)
  - SEO suites (Adobe/Semrush, Similarweb, Ahrefs, Conductor), although these have mostly built
  - Better-funded GEO peers consolidating (Searchable-style; Profound is a possible future acquirer)
  - In Korea and Japan, agencies are currently building, not buying.
- **Playbook signals from the deals:** sell into the likely acquirer as a customer or partner first (XFunnel, Senso). Own a workflow beyond the dashboard (experiments, content/AXP actions, attribution), since monitoring alone is commoditizing. Keep the team small and senior, because acquirers buy the team plus time-to-market.

### Gaps
- No public data on revenue multiples for small GEO deals: XFunnel's and GoShine's ARR at sale are undisclosed (GoShine has only a sponsored "hundreds of thousands" claim).
- The Scrunch earnout and the split between cash and retention are unknown.
- The number of failed or unsold GEO startups is unknown; only Lorelight's shutdown was found, so survivorship bias applies.
