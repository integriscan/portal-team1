## Q1. Who does inspection-to-repair, and which business model works? (player map by model)

### Takeaway
There are three monetization models.
- **SaaS plus a vendor network**, where the network is monetized by fees on provider invoices. ServiceChannel and Corrigo (JLL) use this model.
- **Managed maintenance**, where the company takes the job, subcontracts it and books the gross repair spend as revenue. Vixxo, SMS Assist/Lessen, FacilitySource/CBRE and Lula use this model.
- **Pure CMMS/inspection SaaS**, priced per seat. MaintainX, Limble, UpKeep and SafetyCulture/Mitti use this model.

The large exits so far went to the first and third models. The managed-service players reach large scale, about 2.5M work orders a year at Lessen and SMS Assist, but were valued and sold as services businesses. The 2024–2026 AI-native newcomers (Prefix, Vendoroo, Latchel, Haven, Brickwise, Wayline) mostly sell an AI "coordination layer" between operators and local contractors. Their ARR is small, about $0.6M–$2.5M, and they focus on residential property management. Prefix is the main exception, because it targets multi-site restaurants and retail.

### Inferences
- The only proven way to monetize the repair leg at scale inside a SaaS company is a **small fee on provider invoices**. ServiceChannel's 1.5% capped at $4, and 5% on jobs it sources, is far below marketplace take rates of 10–20%. The buyer pays the SaaS subscription. Given the cap, ServiceChannel's ~$117M of recurring revenue in 2021 was mostly enterprise subscription. This is an inference: the split between client and provider revenue was not disclosed.
- The managed-service model (Vixxo, SMS Assist, Lula, FacilitySource) takes full responsibility for the job and books gross spend. It scales in volume (2.5M work orders a year) but buyers are services and real-estate companies (CBRE, Lessen, PE), not software strategics.
- **Multi-site restaurant and retail is the clearest commercial wedge.** ServiceChannel, Fexa, Ecotrak, Vixxo and Prefix all concentrate on QSR, convenience and retail chains, where refrigeration and HVAC downtime is costly and the buyer is a central facilities team.
- AI-native entrants mostly sell into residential property management, where tenant call volume is high and AI intake pays off quickly. Commercial multi-site AI coordination is less crowded; Prefix is the main funded example found.
- For a 3-person team, the comparable traction points are Prefix (2 years to $2.5M ARR with ~2,000 sites), CentralComs' founder ($600K ARR solo in FM AI) and Brickwise (>$1M contracted ARR, 6 months). Teams of 2–12 at YC are the norm for this category.

### Inferences
- Implied multiples, computed from the cited figures:
  - ServiceChannel: $1.2B / $125M ≈ 9.6x revenue, about 10.3x recurring revenue
  - Brightly: $1.575B / $180M ≈ 8.75x revenue, about 9.8x ARR, before the earn-out
  - MaintainX: $3.6B / $135M ≈ 26.7x 2026E ARR, a premium for >50% growth and the AI/data narrative
- The buyers are industrial and design conglomerates (Fortive, Siemens, Autodesk, Rockwell) building "asset lifecycle" portfolios, plus real-estate services firms (JLL, CBRE, Cushman) buying captive networks.
- Every large target was 8–22 years old at sale. No AI-native maintenance startup has had a large exit yet. The 2025–26 AI deals (Mezo, Twine) are small capability and acqui-hire tuck-ins at undisclosed prices.
- The services businesses changed hands among services players: C&W sold to Vixxo, and SMS Assist merged into Lessen. They did not go to SaaS strategics, which suggests that owning the repair execution lowers the software multiple.

## Q3. What makes inspection-to-repair hard, and what evidence shows AI changing the unit economics?

### Takeaway
The structural hurdles are a vendor network built and policed over decades (ServiceChannel's 70k providers, Vixxo's 150k technicians, Corrigo's 35–56k), weak network credentialing and accountability, and low monetizable take on the repair dollar. Fees are capped at 1.5% or $4 on provider invoices; managed models take more margin but also job risk. AI's measurable effect so far is on the coordination layer, not on the repair itself: vendors report that 6–25% of requests are resolved without a truck roll and that one coordinator can handle 5x more doors.

### Cited Findings
- **Supply density is the moat and the barrier.** ServiceChannel's value was "software with a service-provider network" drawing on "millions of data points" for contractor matching, after more than 20 years of operation. — [SEC Ex. 99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1659166/000119312521213064/d197992dex991.htm). Lessen's pitch for SMS Assist was letting fragmented contractors "act as one, performing work at a national level not previously possible." — [Mann Report](https://www.mannpublications.com/mannreport/2023/01/12/lessen-acquires-sms-assist/)
- **Quality control and accountability gap in pure networks:** a competitor states Corrigo "does not credential, manage, or hold accountability for provider performance." **(snippet; competitor-authored)** — [Lessen battle card](https://lessen.navless.ai/answers/lessen-vs-corrigo-jll-technologies-battle-card)
- **Take-rate ceiling in the SaaS-network model:** 1.5% of approved invoice value capped at $4, and 5% only on network-sourced jobs. Unpaid fees block invoicing, so ServiceChannel uses control of the invoice flow as leverage. **(snippet)** — [ServiceChannel help](https://servicechannel.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/SCUCP/pages/586088951/ServiceChannel+Usage+Fees)
- **Labor shortage as a driver:** Lessen framed the combined platform as helping "owners overcome skilled labor shortages." — [Mann Report](https://www.mannpublications.com/mannreport/2023/01/12/lessen-acquires-sms-assist/)
- **Margin pain for multi-site operators:** Prefix claims multi-location retail and restaurant brands "lose more than 10% of profit margins" to facility-management inefficiency. This is a vendor claim. — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/04/14/prefix-raises-7-5m-scale-ai-driven-facility-management-platform)
- **AI deflection, the share resolved without dispatch (all vendor-reported, residential):**
  - Latchel: 23% "solved at intake," and customers cut dispatches by about 20%. — [latchel.com](https://latchel.com/)
  - HappyCo is skeptical: "only 6-9% of maintenance requests can be truly self-resolved," based on "millions of work orders." — [HappyCo (opened)](https://happy.co/resources/beyond-the-bot)
  - A guide citing Haven puts the figure at about 12–14%. **(snippet)** — [SFAI Labs](https://sfailabs.com/guides/how-ai-triages-maintenance-requests-walkthrough)
- **AI coordinator productivity:** Vendoroo claims 1 coordinator per 200 doors before and at least 1,000 after, with 2 escalations a day per 1,000 doors. — [vendoroo.ai](https://vendoroo.ai/home-page-vendoroo). Latchel claims PMs "double units managed per admin employee." — [latchel.com](https://latchel.com/)
- **AI on cost control and invoice verification:** Property Meld MAX checks each invoice against market rates and claims 18.4% lower repair spend. — [propertymeld.com](https://www.propertymeld.com/). Prefix claims more than 15% cost savings. — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/04/14/prefix-raises-7-5m-scale-ai-driven-facility-management-platform)
- **Inspection data turned into risk pricing (another monetization path):** Mitti (SafetyCulture) prices insurance off inspection data and claims 19% lower claims costs and a 9% better loss ratio for platform users. — [Startland News](https://startlandnews.com/2026/08/safetyculture-mitti/)
- **Inspection-app incumbents are moving toward maintenance:** Mitti's CEO said customers were already "capturing quality issues, maintenance faults" in the app. — [IT Brief](https://itbrief.com.au/story/safetyculture-rebrands-as-mitti-in-frontline-software-push)

### Inferences
- AI economics are clearest in **intake, triage and coordination labor**: deflecting 6–25% of requests and raising coordinator throughput up to 5x (vendor claims). That improves a SaaS or managed-service gross margin but does not remove the need for local vendor density for the other 75–94% of jobs.
- **Payment float and liability matter in the managed model.** Vixxo, Lula and SMS Assist take the job, pay subcontractors and bill clients, so they carry working capital and warranty risk. The SaaS-network model avoids this but monetizes only a capped fee. This is an inference; I found no disclosed DSO or float data.
- Inspection incumbents such as Mitti/SafetyCulture, the HADA analog, already capture "maintenance faults" at scale. A Korean "inspection-to-repair" play differentiates only if it owns the repair leg: vendor matching, completion verification and invoice benchmarking. Owning that leg requires building local vendor density, which every global winner took more than 10 years or an acquisition to get.

### Gaps
- I found no independent, non-vendor study measuring AI deflection rates. All figures are vendor-reported and mostly residential, and none were found for commercial multi-site.
- I found no disclosed take rates or gross margins for managed-maintenance players (Vixxo, Lessen, Lula) and no payment-float or DSO data.
- I found no documented failures or shutdowns of inspection-to-repair startups. Searches for Lessen and SMS Assist layoffs returned nothing relevant. The only distress signal is the aggregator-reported SafetyCulture staff cut in May 2026, which is unverified.
- No liability or insurance-claim data from repair-network operators was found.
