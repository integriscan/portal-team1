## Q1. Global serial acquirers: 2026 M&A priorities and tuck-in patterns

### Takeaway
In 2026 the busiest strategic buyers are enterprise platforms (Salesforce, ServiceNow, Workday, Adobe, Databricks, Snowflake) and security platforms (Palo Alto, CrowdStrike, Zscaler, Cisco). They are mostly buying four things: (1) AI agents that complete a whole workflow (customer service, cross-platform agents, workflow automation), (2) **identity and governance for AI agents** (the most consistent gap across buyers), (3) agent evaluation, observability and security, and (4) data and analytics layers that agents can query. The frontier labs (OpenAI, Anthropic) have become frequent buyers of developer tools, agent infrastructure, AI-security testing and bio/health AI teams.

### Inferences
- The clearest capability gap that buyers keep paying for in 2026 is **identity, governance and security for AI agents (non-human identity)**: Workday/C1.ai, ServiceNow/Veza, Cisco/Astrix, Palo Alto/CyberArk, Koi and Portkey all fit it. A product that secures, authenticates or governs agents is something almost every platform buyer wants, and early product-level startups (Rilo, Astrix) are being bought.
- The second gap is **agent evaluation, observability and testing** (Databricks/Quotient AI, ServiceNow/Traceloop, OpenAI/Promptfoo, Snowflake/Observe). The third is **cross-application agents** that work across competing platforms (ServiceNow/Sweep, Adobe/Rilo).
- Professional-information incumbents (TR, WK, RELX) have suffered sharp stock drops because of AI-native competitors and are building internally; I found no 2026 acquisition evidence for them. They still have a history of buying ($650M for Casetext), so domain-specific agentic tools for tax, audit and legal are a plausible target category. This is an inference, not a stated plan.
- Many of the targets were young (Rilo was about 1 year old), so the bar is a working product that integrates cleanly into the platform, not scale.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm the exact earnings-call wording on tuck-ins for most companies (Salesforce, ServiceNow, SAP, Oracle, Datadog, Veeva, Epic, Shopify, Stripe, Google, Cisco) because transcripts were not reachable. Snippets alone were insufficient.
- No 2026 acquisition data found for SAP, Oracle, Datadog, Veeva, Epic, Shopify or Stripe.
- No published 2026 M&A budgets ($) found for any of these buyers.
- Snowflake's Summit 2026 acquisition target and the Intuit/Sequence deal are unconfirmed.

## Q2. Korean buyers: 2025–2026 strategic priorities and willingness to buy startups

### Takeaway
The Korean conglomerates and big-tech groups have converged on **physical AI and robotics** (Samsung, LG, LG CNS, Hyundai, Hanwha–Krafton), plus fintech (Naver), AI infrastructure (SK) and digital health and HVAC for AI data centers (Samsung). Naver has explicitly said it is looking for M&A in physical AI and fintech, focused on North America and Silicon Valley. Kakao is splitting into a platform company and an M&A/investment holding company with a large capital pool. A 2026 regulatory change allows conglomerates to directly acquire startups their CVCs have invested in, which opens a structural CVC-investment-then-acquisition exit path.

### Inferences
- For a Korean founding team, the most concrete buyer-demand cluster is **physical AI**: robot intelligence, industrial automation, robot software and data. Samsung (RX office), LG (newly hired physical AI chief), LG CNS (unspent ₩200B), Naver and Hanwha–Krafton all name it. ## Q3. Private equity buy-and-build: fragmented industries and multiples

### Takeaway
PE roll-ups are most active in **vertical SaaS** (platforms made up 54% of SaaS M&A in 2Q26) and in services: dental, veterinary, accounting, insurance brokerage and IT MSPs. Small add-ons trade at about 3–5x revenue, or 4–7x EBITDA for profitable slow-growth SaaS. Dental and vet roll-ups are mature, while accounting and MSP roll-ups are mid-cycle. The multiple sources below are mostly advisory blogs, not transaction databases.

### Cited Findings
- Software Equity Group: the 2Q26 median private SaaS deal was about 4.0x trailing revenue (down from 4.2x), and vertical platforms were 54% of SaaS M&A that quarter. Other trackers show 3.1x (Aventis, March 2026) and 5.3x (Windsor Drake). — [Axia Growth](https://axiagrowth.com/resources/industries/technology/vertical-saas); [SaaSrise Q1 2026](https://www.saasrise.com/blog/private-saas-m-a-deals-q1-2026-report)
- Axia notes that no primary source publishes a vertical-only private median or any private multiple for deals under $50M. — [Axia Growth](https://axiagrowth.com/resources/industries/technology/vertical-saas)
- One source puts vertical SaaS medians at about 5.5x and premium verticals at about 7.5x, without naming its dataset. — [SaaSrise vertical report](https://www.saasrise.com/blog/vertical-saas-ma-vc-report-2026)
- PE add-ons price below platform deals, often at 3–5x revenue. — [Windsor Drake](https://windsordrake.com/saas-valuation-multiples/)
- Indicative EBITDA ranges: 4–7x for slow-growth profitable SaaS and 3–6x for services-heavy or weakly recurring software; multiples above 7x are uncommon for small deals. This is one broker's view. — [Breakwater M&A](https://www.breakwaterma.com/ma-market-reports/saas-ma-market-report-2026)
- Public SaaS companies with net retention above 110% traded at a median 8.0x, a 77.8% premium over the 90–110% cohort. — [Axia Growth](https://axiagrowth.com/resources/industries/technology/vertical-saas)
- Service roll-ups (mostly from CT Acquisitions, an advisory blog):
  - Dental: Heartland Dental (KKR / Ontario Teachers') is the largest US DSO, with about 2,500 to 3,000+ affiliated practices (figures conflict).
  - Veterinary: Mars (Banfield, BluePearl, VCA) runs about 2,100+ hospitals. Vet EBITDA multiples are reported at 8–15x or 6–12x (conflicting).
  - Accounting: consolidators include Aprio, Cherry Bekaert, Eisner and Citrin Cooperman. It is a mid-stage roll-up with expanding multiples; bolt-ons trade at 4–8x EBITDA.
  - Insurance brokerage: the most mature roll-up. Gallagher closed its $13.45B acquisition of AssuredPartners on 2025-08-18.
  - IT MSPs: consolidators include ConvergeOne, Evergreen Services Group, New Era Technology and Logically (Riverside). Multiples are 5–9x or 6–9x EBITDA; mid-cycle in 2026, with modest compression expected over the next 24–36 months.
  — [CT Acquisitions, fragmented industries](https://ctacquisitions.com/fragmented-industries-ripe-for-consolidation-2026/); [CT Acquisitions, 2026 PE platforms by sector](https://ctacquisitions.com/guides/private-equity-platforms-by-sector-2026/); [CT Acquisitions, roll-up guide](https://ctacquisitions.com/roll-up-strategy-guide-2026/); [Dealroom](https://dealroom.net/blog/roll-up-strategy)
- Trend: vertical SaaS companies are starting to buy the service businesses they used to sell into (SaaS plus services roll-ups). — [SaaS Intelligence](https://saasintelligence.substack.com/p/vertical-saas-is-buying-the-market)

### Inferences
- For a small founding team, PE-backed vertical SaaS platforms are a realistic buyer if the startup has an AI add-on module with recurring revenue (for accounting, dental, vet, MSP or insurance-agency workflows). Pricing will be around 3–5x revenue, so it is a "modest but likely" exit. Net retention above 110% supports a premium.
- Accounting roll-ups (PE-backed CPA firms) are mid-cycle, and their consolidators need AI to standardize and automate back-office work across acquired firms. That makes them potential customers and indirect buyers of AI tools for that work.

### Gaps

## Q5. Typical sizes and multiples for small AI startups (acqui-hire per engineer, ARR multiples)

### Takeaway
2026 acqui-hire pricing is usually expressed per engineer: roughly $0.8–1.5M for senior SWEs, $1.5–3M for ML/AI engineers and $3–5M+ for LLM/RLHF specialists. A 6-person AI team typically lands at about $8–25M in total consideration. Headline prices overstate what founders receive because of liquidation preferences and retention packages. AI-native targets get premiums over general SaaS, but I found no reliable ARR-multiple data for small AI startups.

### Cited Findings
- ValueAddVC (June 2026): senior SWE $800K–1.5M, ML/AI engineers $1.5–3M, LLM/RLHF specialists $3–5M+ per head. A 2026 6-person AI team commands $15–25M, versus $5–8M for a comparable SaaS team in 2019. — [ValueAddVC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/acqui-hire-deals-how-theyre-structured-and-what-founders-actually-get)
- Cooley GO: from a few hundred thousand to $2M per head (based on companies that had mostly raised under $5M). — [Cooley GO](https://www.cooleygo.com/acqui-hire-basics/)
- A startup lawyer puts distressed deals at a few hundred thousand to about $2M per head, and about $8–16M total as an opening position for a 6-person AI team; new-hire packages for senior engineers can run well into seven figures. — [Louis Lehot Substack](https://louislehotattorney.substack.com/p/what-an-acqui-hire-looks-like-for)
- Startups.com: $1–5M per engineer, and $5–10M+ for senior AI researchers in 2024–2025 (examples: Inflection to Microsoft, Adept to Amazon, Character.AI to Google). — [Startups.com](https://www.startups.com/lexicon/acqui-hire)
- Founder proceeds: in a $10M deal after a $4M seed with a 1x preference, founders get about $3.3M. — [ValueAddVC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/acqui-hire-deals-how-theyre-structured-and-what-founders-actually-get)
- Small but strategic AI deal sizes in 2026: Stainless (>$300M), Astrix (about $400M), Sweep (about $400M estimated), Intuit/Sequence (about $200M reported), Coefficient Bio ($400M or $4B, conflicting). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/18/anthropic-has-acquired-the-dev-tools-startup-used-by-openai-google-and-cloudflare/); [FE International](https://www.feinternational.com/blog/cybersecurity-ma); [SalesforceDevops.net](https://salesforcedevops.net/index.php/2026/09/08/the-400-million-admin-tool/); [Angular Ventures](https://newsletter.angularventures.com/p/running-to-stay-in-place)
- FE International argues that AI acquirers pay premium multiples (advisory blog; a "10–18x AI-native" range circulates secondhand, so verify it). — [FE International AI M&A](https://www.feinternational.com/blog/ai-ma-trend)
- Reference point: Filevine was valued at a reported $3B, or 16x ARR on about $187M ARR (September 2025). — [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/250m-year-notion-of-personal-injury-litigation/)

### Inferences
- A small AI team that is acquired without meaningful ARR will likely be priced per head ($1–3M per AI engineer). With $1–10M ARR and strategic fit (identity, security, evaluation, vertical workflows), outcomes in the $100–400M range have occurred in 2026, but those were security and developer-tools companies with strong technical moats.
- When pricing is per head, team composition (number of ML/LLM specialists) is the main value driver, so the founding team structure should be planned with that in mind.

### Gaps
- No reliable transaction-database figures found for ARR multiples of small (under $10M ARR) AI startups.
- No Korean acqui-hire per-head pricing data found.
