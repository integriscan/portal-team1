## Q1. Case studies: tiny-team startups acquired 2024–2026 within ~1–3 years of founding

### Takeaway
The standout case is Base44: a solo, bootstrapped founder built it with Cursor and sold it to Wix about 6 months after starting, for about $80M upfront plus earn-outs. Those earn-outs have since pushed his proceeds past $150M, as Base44 grew to $100M–$200M ARR inside Wix. Most other tiny-team AI exits in 2025–2026 were acqui-hires or "team + tech" deals with undisclosed terms, often followed by a product shutdown. Base44's price came from viral distribution plus fast-growing paying revenue in a category a strategic buyer feared, not from team size alone.

### Cited Findings

**Base44 → Wix (June 2025), the benchmark case**
- Wix agreed to buy Base44 in June 2025 for an initial payment of about $80M, with further milestone payments running to 2029 — [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001513267); described as an all-cash $80M deal — [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/wix-buys-ai-startup-base44-for-80m-six-month-sprint-deal); earn-outs noted — [PrivSource](https://www.privsource.com/acquisitions/deal/X0SzR7)
- Conflicting deal-value report: one source says it was about $92M, with only $18M in cash near signing and the rest contingent on targets — [Globes, earn-out article](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001542919) (per search extract; conflicts with the "$80M all-cash" framing above)
- Founder Maor Shlomo was a solo, bootstrapped founder who started Base44 as a side project with no outside funding — [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/wix-buys-ai-startup-base44-for-80m-six-month-sprint-deal)
- Reported team size at the deal conflicts: 6 employees, all hired in the month before — [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001513267); 8 employees sharing $25M in retention bonuses — [DesignRush](https://news.designrush.com/wix-buys-ai-startup-base44-for-80m-six-month-sprint-deal), with about 8 also per [PrivSource](https://www.privsource.com/acquisitions/deal/X0SzR7)
- Time to exit was about 6 months, but reports differ on the start date (January 2025 vs. October 2024) — [intro.co](https://intro.co/blog/how-base44-got-acquired-in-500-days); [whatastartup Substack](https://whatastartup.substack.com/p/a-solo-founder-just-sold-his-6-months-old-ai-startup-for-80-million-dollars)
- Traction at the deal: about $189K net profit in May 2025, after LLM costs — [Website Planet](https://www.websiteplanet.com/news/wix-acquires-base44); 250K–300K users within weeks, including B2B users such as eToro and SimilarWeb; grown without paid marketing, mainly through the founder building in public on LinkedIn and X — [intro.co](https://intro.co/blog/how-base44-got-acquired-in-500-days); 10,000+ businesses and 1,300+ paying customers per one report — [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001513267). ARR at closing is described only as "a few million"; no reliable exact figure was found.
- The founder bootstrapped with about $10K–20K and used Cursor as his development environment — [whatastartup Substack](https://whatastartup.substack.com/p/a-solo-founder-just-sold-his-6-months-old-ai-startup-for-80-million-dollars) (secondary source)
- Why he sold: Shlomo said he couldn't organically reach the scale he needed, and Wix offered distribution and resources — [intro.co](https://intro.co/blog/how-base44-got-acquired-in-500-days)
- After the deal: Wix paid Shlomo about $38M in shares with its Q1 2026 results after Base44 hit its sales targets, then said it would pay another $41M after Q2 2026 milestones. Total proceeds are reported at over $150M — [Globes](https://en.globes.co.il/en/article-1001542919). Base44 ARR reportedly passed $100M in March 2026 and approached $150M by mid-May 2026 — [Shopifreaks](https://www.shopifreaks.com/wix-stock-plunges-27-on-q1-return-to-losses-despite-base44-ai-platform-hitting-150m-arr-ahead-of-internal-targets/). A later, undated report puts it at $200M ARR (via Calcalist search results, not verified).

### Inferences
- Base44 is a positive outlier, not a base rate. It combined consumer-grade virality, early profitability, a category that threatened the acquirer's core business (Wix website and app building), and a strategic buyer with distribution. A 3-person team targeting a fast exit should look for a buyer whose core product its own product threatens or completes.
- Most of the price in the Base44 deal ended up in performance-based earn-outs. A fast exit in 2025–2026 often means 2–4 more years tied to the acquirer, not a clean cash-out.
- Small "team + tech" acquisitions (Dashworks, Convergence, Bud, Roi) usually come with undisclosed, likely modest terms and a product shutdown. They are realistic outcomes for a 3-person team, but they are closer to acqui-hire pricing than strategic pricing.

### Gaps
- No confirmed 2025–2026 acquisitions of tiny AI-agent startups by Atlassian, Canva or Notion turned up. Figma/Weavy and Canva/MagicBrief could not be verified in this session.
- No primary-source evidence was found of a specific exit attributed to building with Claude Code. Tool-usage claims (Cursor for Base44) come from secondary newsletters.
- No Bolt.new/StackBlitz or Rork acquisition was found.
- Exact ARR at Base44's closing is not public.

## Q2. What acquirers value in small AI startups now, and the pitfalls

### Takeaway
Advisory sources agree that buyers pay for proprietary data and data pipelines, deep workflow embedding in a vertical, distribution and customer relationships, and scarce teams. Thin wrappers over third-party models get the lowest multiples, and contract terms on data rights now matter in diligence. Pure acqui-hires are a narrower and more variable market than the headlines suggest. Most of these sources are consultants and advisors, so their multiples are indicative only.

### Cited Findings
- Proprietary data and the pipelines that feed it are the most cited value driver; buyers want "data a buyer can't assemble from public sources" — [FE International](https://www.feinternational.com/blog/what-do-acquirers-look-for-in-an-ai-company-in-fy-2027); [Lily Wolstenholme Substack](https://lilywolstenholme.substack.com/p/how-proprietary-data-is-changing)
- Thin wrappers get the bottom of their valuation band regardless of growth — [FE International, AI M&A trends](https://www.feinternational.com/blog/ai-ma-trend); one advisor puts GPT wrappers at 2–5x ARR vs. 15–25x ARR for AI infrastructure with $3M+ ARR — [733 Park](https://www.733park.com/guides/how-ai-companies-are-valued-for-acquisition/) (unverified advisory figures)
- Applied AI with proprietary data sells at 6–12x ARR in a private process, and strategic buyers who need the data, workflow position or team can pay well above that — [Mooning](https://mooning.com/blog/tech-ma-2026-what-buyers-pay-for) (advisory)
- Workflow embedding matters because an incumbent can push the product through its existing customer base — [Kanopy Labs](https://kanopylabs.com/blog/positioning-startup-for-ai-era-acquisition)
- Acqui-hires: one source cites $5M to $50M+ per engineer for exceptional pre-revenue teams, with wide variance; another calls pure acqui-hires a smaller and more variable market — [Karich & Associates](https://www.karich.net/blog/big-tech-startup-acquisition-ip-vs-acqui-hire); [FE International](https://www.feinternational.com/blog/ai-ma-trend). Retention risk feeds into price: one source reports 33% of acquired-startup staff left within a year, vs. 12% of regular hires — [BeyondElevation](https://beyondelevation.com/insights/acquirer-premium-multiples-ai-startups)
- IP and contract diligence: buyers ask whether customer agreements allow training on customer data and whether those rights transfer on a change of control — [FE International](https://www.feinternational.com/blog/what-do-acquirers-look-for-in-an-ai-company-in-fy-2027). Documented IP is claimed to add a premium of 15–20% (or 30–50% in one anecdote) — [BeyondElevation](https://beyondelevation.com/insights/ai-wrapper-ip-strategy-defensibility-2026) (single-source, unverified)
- Platform risk, historical evidence: ChatGPT's PDF-upload feature (November 2023) hit "chat with PDF" wrapper startups — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/06/get-the-pdf-outta-here); Sam Altman warned that OpenAI would "steamroll" startups that merely wrap GPT-4 — [TechStartups](https://techstartups.com/?p=85972)
- Buyer caution: one in five strategic buyers reportedly walked away from a 2025 deal over concerns about AI exposure (citing L40's SaaS Multiples report) — [Fungies](https://fungies.io/saas-valuation-statistics-2026/) (secondary)
- Distribution motive in practice: Wix bought Base44 partly because the founder could not reach scale alone and Wix could supply distribution — [intro.co](https://intro.co/blog/how-base44-got-acquired-in-500-days)

### Inferences
- For a 3-person team, the product most likely to be acquired is one that (a) sits inside a buyer's ecosystem, such as a plugin or integration for HubSpot, Salesforce or Wix-type platforms, (b) builds a proprietary data asset or workflow lock-in in a vertical, and (c) shows paying revenue. A general-purpose wrapper anyone can copy invites a feature launch from the model provider and gets low multiples.
- Contract hygiene from day one (data-rights clauses, IP assignment among the 3 founders, clean cap table) is cheap and directly affects the price and speed of diligence.

### Gaps
- No independent, transaction-level dataset was found that separates "wrapper" from "proprietary data" AI deal multiples. Figures come from advisors.
- No 2025-specific documented case was found of a model-provider feature launch killing a named startup.

## Q3. Micro-acquisition market data (Acquire.com / Flippa / Microns, 2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Small-business marketplaces price on profit, not hype. Acquire.com reports a median confirmed SaaS profit multiple of about 3.9x in both 2024 and 2025, for deals under $10M enterprise value. Flippa's micro-SaaS averages are lower (about 2.85x profit) and its AI app sales are small (about $536K average). On these marketplaces a 3-person team's realistic outcome is mid six to low seven figures, which requires solid profit and usually 12+ months of revenue history.

### Cited Findings
- Acquire.com's January 2026 biannual report: median confirmed SaaS profit multiple of 3.9x in both 2024 and 2025, covering businesses under $10M enterprise value — [Acquire.com blog](https://blog.acquire.com/acquire-com-biannual-acquisition-multiples-report-jan-2026/). The PDF breaks out 3.7x for deals under $100K and 3.9x for $100K–$1M — [Acquire Multiples Report 2025 PDF](https://blog.acquire.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Acquire-Multiples-Report-2025.pdf) (search extracts; pages not fetchable in this session)
- Average confirmed multiples were in the low-to-mid 4x range, with average margins around 71% in 2025 — [Acquire.com SaaS multiples guide](https://blog.acquire.com/saas-valuation-multiples/)
- Acquire.com moved to profit-based multiples in January 2024. An earlier average confirmed SaaS multiple was 4.3x profit, with a range of 0.63x to 34x — [Livmo summary](https://livmo.com/blog/acquire-com-biannual-acquisition-multiples-report-feb-2023-livmo-summary/) (dating inconsistent)
- Acquire.com deals typically run $50K–$3M, and buyers typically want 12+ months of clean revenue history — [whatastartup / search extract](https://whatastartup.substack.com/p/a-solo-founder-just-sold-his-6-months-old-ai-startup-for-80-million-dollars) (secondary; attribution in the search extract was imprecise)
- Analysis of more than 600 live 2026 marketplace listings, including Acquire.com: the smallest deals ask about 2.6x trailing revenue and 10.7x trailing profit (these are asking prices, not closed deals; source text partly garbled) — [Beancount.io](https://beancount.io/de/blog/2026/07/11/bootstrapped-saas-valuation-multiples-2026-acquire-com-indie-founders-guide)
- Flippa data: micro-SaaS sells at about 2.85x annual profit on average, with the top quartile at 6.13x — [Startupa.ge](https://startupa.ge/blog/how-to-sell-micro-saas); micro-SaaS under $1M ARR typically sells at 3–5x annual profit on marketplaces — [Startupa.ge valuation guide](https://startupa.ge/blog/how-much-is-my-business-worth)
- Flippa AI segment: 14 AI app and tool sales averaging $535,714, with the average AI business about 2.5 years old — [Domainsmoke Flippa review 2026](https://www.domainsmoke.com/blog/flippa-review-2026-what-the-top-marketplace-for-online-businesses-offers-right-now). Flippa's 2025 recap says SaaS transactions rose sharply and buyers repriced digital businesses around AI — [Flippa 2025 recap](https://flippa.com/blog/2025-online-business-ma-insights-from-flippa/). Its Q2 2026 report sorts listings into AI-native, AI-resilient and AI-exposed groups — [Flippa Q2 2026](https://flippa.com/blog/digital-ma-market-trends-2026-q2/). Flippa marketing also claims AI companies trade at 25–30x revenue vs. about 6x for SaaS, a headline benchmark rather than a marketplace transaction average — [Flippa AI valuation](https://flippa.com/blog/how-to-value-an-ai-company-7-key-metrics/)
- Example Flippa listing: an AI SaaS with $70K ARR, 93% margins and zero churn — [Flippa listing](https://flippa.com/11970430-ai-saas-with-93-margins-70k-arr-zero-churn-and-multinational-clients-locked-in-through-2026highly-automated-and-ready-to-scale-with-sales-focus)
- Microns, a micro-startup marketplace, facilitated over $200K in acquisitions in about 8 months after launching Offers in 2025, after GMV of about $50K in 2023 and about $100K in 2024 — [Microns 2025 Year in Review](https://newsletter.microns.io/p/2025-year-in-review)
- Private SaaS benchmark: median 4.8–5.3x ARR in 2025 (citing SaaS Capital) — [Startupa.ge](https://startupa.ge/blog/how-much-is-my-business-worth); AI-core SaaS at about 12.5x EV/Revenue (January 2026 analysis, source unclear) — [Fungies](https://fungies.io/saas-valuation-statistics-2026/)

### Inferences
- Rough marketplace math: about $20K/month net profit is about $240K/year, which at 3.9x is about $0.9M. Reaching a $5M exit on a marketplace needs about $1.3M of annual profit. Marketplaces suit a modest "fallback" exit. Life-changing outcomes come from strategic buyers who pay for distribution or category fit (as with Base44), not from profit multiples.
- Marketplace AI businesses selling at about 2.5 years old fits the team's 2–3-year horizon, but at small prices.

### Gaps
- No per-category breakdown of "what sells fastest" or time-to-close from Acquire.com 2025–2026 was obtained. The report pages could not be fetched.
- No AI-specific median multiple from Acquire.com was found.
- Microns publishes no multiple statistics.

## Q4. Korean exit environment for small startups

### Takeaway
Korea's venture exit market depends heavily on IPOs and secondary sales. True M&A exits are a low single-digit share of recoveries by parliamentary-audit figures (about 2.3% in 2026 vs. 47.3% in the US). Government policy is incremental: it subsidizes M&A costs (valuation, diligence, PMI) and has loosened venture-fund sale rules, but no new tax incentive for 2025–2026 could be confirmed. Documented Korean acqui-hires of AI teams and small-startup sale prices are scarce in public reporting. Cross-border sale to Japanese buyers is rare; Korean AI firms instead enter Japan through partnerships and listings (for example, Allganize).

### Cited Findings
- **Exit mix:**
  - IPOs were 49.1% of venture recoveries by amount in 2024 — [머니투데이](https://www.mt.co.kr/future/2025/12/03/2025120219494184528)
  - "Sales" (M&A, buyouts and secondary purchases combined) fell from 54.4% in the prior year to 37.9% in January–May 2025, while IPOs rose to 46.7% — [아시아경제](https://core.asiae.co.kr/article/2025080815381024138) (search extract; attribution between the 머니투데이 and 아시아경제 articles was not fully separable)
  - IPOs were 40.6% in January–May 2026 — [한국경제](https://www.hankyung.com/article/2026071325041)
- **2025 recoveries (2026 parliamentary audit):** startup recoveries were ₩3.0893T in 2025, up about 83% from ₩1.69T in 2024. IPOs were 37.3% of that, with IPO recoveries rising from ₩410B to ₩1.1537T. Korea's M&A recovery share was essentially flat, from 2.1% in 2014 to 2.3% in 2026, vs. 47.3% in the US — [매일일보 (2026 국감)](https://www.m-i.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=2009662); [에너지데일리](https://www.energydaily.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=204353); [뉴스1](https://www.news1.kr/industry/sb-founded/6330307) (figures from search extracts across these articles; verify which outlet carries each number)
- **Funding for the M&A/secondary channel:** the fund-of-funds commitment to M&A and secondary funds fell from ₩115B (2023) to ₩47.5B (2024) and was only ₩60B in 2025 — [에너지데일리](https://www.energydaily.co.kr/news/articleView.html?idxno=204353) (search extract). The minister cited a culture unfriendly to M&A — [뉴스1](https://www.news1.kr/industry/sb-founded/6330307)
- **Venture investment backdrop:** 2025 venture investment was the second-highest on record and the number of unicorns reached 27 — [네이트뉴스](https://m.news.nate.com/view/20260213n24570). Outstanding venture investment hit a record ₩32T with "money not coming back out" — [유니콘팩토리](https://www.unicornfactory.co.kr/article/2025112118203257607)
- **Policy:**
  - February 2025: an amended enforcement decree let venture investment funds sell portfolio stakes to major LPs and affiliates without unanimous LP consent, unless the terms are unfavorable to the fund — [MTN](https://news.mtn.co.kr/news-detail/2025022512382254670)
  - 2025 M&A support program (announced March 26, 2025): KIBO's public-private M&A platform launched, the existing deal network became an information-only "M&A 정보망", and valuation costs for sell-side firms are subsidized — [벤처스퀘어](https://www.venturesquare.net/962685/)
  - 2026 M&A support program (announced March 2026): adds diligence and PMI subsidies on top of valuation-cost support. Integrated due diligence is subsidized up to ₩30M, per-area legal/accounting/tax diligence at 50% up to ₩10M, and PMI consulting at 50% up to ₩25M — [시대일보](https://www.sidae.com/article/2026031214230657245)
  - Tax: the historic "technology-innovation M&A" incentive (a 2013 plan offering a corporate tax deduction of 10% of the technology value) is past policy. Current 2026 tax-credit terms could not be confirmed — [아주경제 (2013)](https://www.ajunews.com/view/20131018000273)
- **Korean M&A activity examples:** 데이원컴퍼니 acquired the AX startup 스페이스와이 in August 2026, framed as a "Palantir-style" growth engine — [플래텀](https://platum.kr/archives/292527). A Korean exchange-related AI startup acquisition was also reported — [딜사이트](https://dealsite.co.kr/articles/155050) (details not retrieved). Earlier 100% acquisitions of small startups include 핀다→오픈업 — [플래텀](https://platum.kr/archives/190100). - **Acqui-hire regulation:** the KFTC has moved to treat AI talent "poaching" (acqui-hires) as a business combination subject to merger review — [한국M&A경제신문](https://www.kmnanews.com/news/articleView.html?idxno=10350); [주간경향](https://weekly.khan.co.kr/article/202610100600011/)
- **Cross-border:**
  - Korean-founded enterprise AI firm Allganize is set to list in Japan, where its Japanese business is now its main market — [KED Global](https://www.kedglobal.com/upcoming-ipos/newsView/ked202609300007)

  - Krafton bought Japan's ADK for ¥75B ($517M), Korea buying Japan — [KED Global](https://www.kedglobal.com/games/newsView/ked202506240006)

### Inferences
- For a Korean 3-person team, a domestic M&A exit within 2–3 years is structurally hard: buyers are few, the culture is IPO-centric, and policy offers only cost subsidies. The highest-probability path to a fast exit is to (a) build for a global or US market from day one so the team is visible to global strategic buyers (as Base44 was to Wix), and/or (b) target Korean mid- and large-cap buyers doing AX (AI transformation) acquisitions (the 데이원컴퍼니 pattern), at modest prices.
- Government M&A subsidies (up to about ₩30M diligence and ₩25M PMI) help lower transaction costs once a buyer exists. They do not create buyers.

## Q5. Japan as a buyer of Korean or small startups (brief)

### Takeaway
Japan has a very large SME-succession M&A market (about 13,000–14,000 domestic deals in 2025) and a new "AI × succession" roll-up thesis, but evidence of Japanese corporates buying Korean AI startups is absent. For a Korean team, Japan currently looks more like a customer and partnership market than an exit market.
