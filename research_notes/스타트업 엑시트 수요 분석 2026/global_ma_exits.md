## Q1. Aggregate data: M&A deal counts by sector, 2025 and Q1–Q3 2026 (Crunchbase, PitchBook-NVCA, CB Insights, Carta, Dealroom, sector trackers)

### Takeaway
M&A **value** reached record highs in 2025–2026: Crunchbase counts about 2,300 VC-backed M&A deals worth $214B+ in 2025, and PitchBook puts US VC-backed acquisition value at a decade-high $465.3B for 9M 2026. Deal **count** is flat to falling: CB Insights says Q3'26 global M&A fell to 2,353 deals, a four-year low. By deal count, the most active categories that can be documented are: (1) **AI-on-AI acquisitions**, with 195 through Sept 29, 2026, already 14% above all of 2025; (2) **cybersecurity**, with a record 219 deals in H1'26 per Momentum Cyber and 420+ in 2025 per SecurityWeek; (3) **digital health**, with 195 US deals in 2025 and 115 in H1'26 per Rock Health; (4) **fintech**, with 230 deals in Q3'26 per CB Insights, down 32% YoY; (5) **vertical SaaS**, at 54% of SaaS M&A in Q2'26 per SEG, mostly PE add-ons. None of the major data vendors publishes a single cross-sector ranking by deal count that I could access.

### Inferences
- Ranked by documented **deal count**, the leading categories are roughly: AI/ML (AI applications plus AI dev tools; 195 AI-on-AI deals before counting big-tech buyers), then cybersecurity (about 420–440 per year across all buyers, including PE), digital health (about 195 per year in the US), fintech (about 230 per quarter globally per CB Insights) and vertical SaaS (the majority of SaaS M&A, mostly PE add-ons). Trackers use different methods, so these numbers should not be added together or ranked strictly.
- The 2026 market is barbell-shaped. Mega-deal value hit records (Cursor, Wiz, Hugging Face, OpenRouter), while overall deal count declined to a four-year low per CB Insights. That suggests larger, AI-driven consolidation and fewer mid-tier exits.
- Legal AI (Legora, Harvey) and healthcare AI are the clearest verticals where funded AI startups have become serial acquirers. This is a demand signal for small vertical-AI teams.
- About 80% of acquired startups raised under $50M (Carta, secondary). Most exits are small tuck-ins rather than headline deals.

### Gaps
- I could not access a single PitchBook, CB Insights or Crunchbase table ranking **all** sectors by M&A deal count for 2025 or 2026; the full reports are paywalled or fetching was blocked. The sector counts above come from separate sector-specific trackers.
- No Carta industry-level M&A breakdown was found. The SVB/HSBC Innovation Banking State of the Markets M&A sector data was not found in this pass.
- No count-based data was found for martech, insurtech, defense tech or data infrastructure specifically.
- PitchBook's Q3'26 deal count is unresolved (5,012 vs 3,640+ in secondary sources).

## Q3. Trends: acqui-hires / license-and-hire, young AI teams acquired fast, cyber consolidation, PE vertical-SaaS add-ons

### Takeaway
Four trends are well supported. (1) "License-and-hire" or reverse-acquihire structures have become a standard AI exit route; Nvidia–Groq at about $20B is the largest. (2) AI labs and funded AI startups have become serial acquirers of small teams. (3) Cybersecurity is consolidating at record deal counts, with PE heavily involved. (4) PE add-ons dominate vertical SaaS M&A.

### Cited Findings
- **License-and-hire:** Nvidia–Groq (Dec 2025, about $20B) was a non-exclusive license plus a leadership hire. It mirrors the structures Microsoft, Amazon and Google used (e.g., Google's about $3B Character.ai deal, which drew a DOJ review but no action). It was chosen because a full acquisition "might take a year or two" and face antitrust risk. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/12/24/nvidia-buying-ai-chip-startup-groq-for-about-20-billion-biggest-deal.html); [Michael Parekh](https://michaelparekh.substack.com/p/ai-nvidia-strikes-a-big-ai-acqui); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/nvidia-license-groq-technology-20-123939149.html)
- Google–Windsurf ($2.4B license plus CEO hire, Jul 2025) is the other key example. Analysts call these deals "increasingly common" as a way to avoid regulators. Meta–Scale AI is cited as an earlier example. — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/google-acqui-hires-windsurf-boost-ai-coding-amid-tech-talent-war); [DeepLearning.AI](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/google-cognition-carve-up-windsurf-after-openais-failed-3b-acquisition-bid)
- **Acqui-hires by AI labs:** OpenAI's 2026 deals include several team-only acqui-hires: Convogo (Jan), OpenClaw creator (Feb) and Hiro Finance (Apr). — [TechCrunch – Convogo](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/08/openai-to-acquire-the-team-behind-executive-coaching-ai-tool-convogo); [ai2.work](https://ai2.work/blog/openai-closes-six-acquisitions-as-ai-lab-consolidation-heats-up)
- Crunchbase (2025) noted that startups buying startups was growing, driven by AI and fintech, with acqui-hires a key motive. — [Crunchbase News – Why more startups are buying startups](https://news.crunchbase.com/ma/startup-acquisitions-acquihire-growing-ai-fintech-2025/)
- **Serial AI acquirers:** 195 AI-on-AI deals were completed by Sept 29, 2026, while the buyer pool grew only 2%. Concentration is in OpenAI (10), Anthropic (5), Legora (5) and Harvey (4). Four AI labs each made an acquisition in a single week in May 2026. — [Crunchbase News](https://news.crunchbase.com/ma/ai-startup-acquisitions-legal-healthcare-openai/); [StartupHub](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/ai-news/2026/four-labs-four-acquisitions-ai-consolidation-may-2026)
- **Young AI startups acquired quickly:** OpenRouter was bought by Stripe less than 3 months after raising at a $1.3B valuation. — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/stripe-openrouter-acquisition-confirmed). Astral, founded 2022–23, was bought in March 2026. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/03/19/openai-to-acquire-developer-tooling-startup-astral.html)
- **Cyber consolidation:** record H1'26 count of 219 deals (Momentum Cyber); PE made 165 cyber deals in 2025 and 89 in H1'26; 8 cyber deals were over $1B in 2025. Platform buyers include Google (Wiz), ServiceNow (Armis, Veza), Palo Alto (CyberArk, Chronosphere) and Accenture (Dragos). — [Pluang/Momentum Cyber](https://pluang.com/en/news-feed/tinjauan-pertengahan-tahun-2026-momentum-cyber-m-a-keamanan-siber); [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/8-cybersecurity-acquisitions-surpassed-1-billion-mark-in-2025/)
- **PE / vertical SaaS:** add-ons were about 72% of PE deal volume in Q1'26 and vertical platforms 54% of SaaS M&A in Q2'26. Vertical SaaS companies are "buying the market" with AI tuck-ins. — [SaaSRise](https://www.saasrise.com/blog/private-saas-m-a-deals-q1-2026-report); [Axia Growth/SEG](https://axiagrowth.com/resources/industries/technology/vertical-saas); [SaaS Intelligence](https://saasintelligence.substack.com/p/vertical-saas-is-buying-the-market)
- **Discounted exits of 2021-vintage SaaS:** Airtable and Miro sold at nearly 90% discounts to their peak valuations (PitchBook-NVCA Q3'26 commentary). — [The Afterburner](https://theafterburner.substack.com/p/record-dollars-rare-exits)
- **H2 2026 slowdown signal:** CB Insights shows Q3'26 M&A at a four-year low. A Crunchbase-based summary shows fewer than 100 startup-on-startup deals in H2 vs 440+ in H1. — [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/research/report/state-of-venture-q326/); [valueaddvc](https://valueaddvc.com/pulse/openai-anthropic-unicorn-acquisitions-wave-2026)

### Inferences
- For startup exit planning, the most liquid buyer segments in 2026 are AI labs and AI unicorns buying small teams (dev tools, evals/security, vertical apps), cyber platform consolidators, and PE-backed vertical SaaS platforms doing AI tuck-ins.
- License-and-hire deals pay founders and key staff but may leave investors and remaining employees with less. This structure is now standard for AI talent deals with antitrust risk.
- Exit values are bifurcated: strategic AI-infra assets get large premiums, while 2021-era horizontal SaaS sells at deep discounts.

### Gaps

- PE add-on share specifically for VC-backed startup targets (as opposed to all SaaS) was not found in Crunchbase or PitchBook data accessible here.
