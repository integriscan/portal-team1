## Q1. Which startups are in each sub-segment, and what is their status / funding / positioning?

### Takeaway
The space is crowded and consolidating fast: in the ~12 months to Oct 2026, at least 10 agent-identity/agent-security/agent-observability startups were acquired or signed to be acquired (Veza, Astrix, Oasis, Entro, C1/ConductorOne, Natoma, Stytch, Portkey, Langfuse, Arize), while the remaining independents raised large 2026 rounds (Zenity $125M, Onyx $113M, Straiker $64M, Arcade $60M, AIR $50M, Runlayer $30M, Geordie $30M, Lasso $30M). NHI/agent-identity is the most consolidated segment; MCP gateways/tool-auth and runtime "agent control" are the most actively funded and newest.

## Q3. Where is crowding most severe, and what sub-niches look underserved?

### Takeaway
Crowding is worst in (1) enterprise NHI/agent-identity discovery (now mostly absorbed by incumbents), (2) enterprise agent runtime security/"guardian agents" (heavily funded Israeli/US players), and (3) MCP gateways (dozens of commercial + OSS + incumbent options). Visibly thinner areas are: compliance-grade audit evidence (per-user, tamper-evident, mapped to SOC 2/HIPAA/EU AI Act/local rules), agent-to-agent delegation chains, SMB/mid-market packaging, and non-US/Asian markets (Korea/Japan) where search found essentially no local startups.

### Cited Findings
**Crowding signals**
- Identity is called the most crowded agent-security layer in vendor maps. — [wetheflywheel](https://wetheflywheel.com/en/guides/ai-agent-security-vendors-2026/)
- Analysts note standalone NHI vendors face a narrowing market as agent governance folds into identity platforms (in Cisco–Astrix coverage). — [Security Boulevard](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/05/cisco-fortifies-ai-defenses-with-acquisition-of-astrix-security/)
- Gartner published its first Market Guide for Guardian Agents (Feb 25, 2026), calling the market early and "chaotic"; Avivah Litan: vendors moving into guardian agents are "ripe targets for acquisition"; Gartner forecasts 5–7% of agentic AI spend on guardian agents by 2028. — [The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/03/5-learnings-from-first-ever-gartner.html); [IT Brew](https://www.itbrew.com/stories/2026/03/06/guardian-agents-are-here-and-ready-to-disrupt-the-market)

### Inferences
- A horizontal "agent permissions + audit trail" product for US enterprise is a red ocean: incumbents (Okta/Auth0, Cisco, SailPoint, ServiceNow, Workday, Palo Alto, CrowdStrike) plus 20+ funded startups.
- Most promising wedges for a 3-person team appear to be: (1) **compliance-evidence layer** — tamper-evident, per-human-principal agent action logs mapped to specific frameworks (e.g., Korea's 개인정보보호법/ISMS-P, Japan APPI, EU AI Act Art. 12 logging, financial-sector rules), sold to auditors/GRC rather than SecOps; (2) **non-US market localization** (Korea/Japan), where no local startups surfaced and incumbents are only signing MoUs; (3) **SMB/mid-market self-serve** packaging (most players sell enterprise); (4) **agent-to-agent delegation provenance**, which is standards-immature (risky but differentiated).
- Exit environment is favorable (many acquirers), but acquirers have bought companies with $25–200M raised and enterprise logos; a tiny team would need a sharp niche or regional dominance to be relevant.

### Gaps
- No public pricing found for most enterprise players (Zenity, Onyx, Noma, Runlayer, Keycard, Aembit); only Arcade and Composio developer pricing surfaced.
- No quantitative data on SMB vs enterprise customer mix in this market.
- Korea/Japan: absence of evidence in English/Korean/Japanese search snippets is not proof of absence; local startups (e.g., Korean security vendors adding MCP features) may exist but were not found. hankyung.com etc. not checked.
