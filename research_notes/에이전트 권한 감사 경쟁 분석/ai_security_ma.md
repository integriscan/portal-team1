# M&A in AI Security, AI-Agent Security, NHI, Agent Identity/Authorization and MCP Security (Jan 2024 – Oct 2026)

> **Method note (read first).** Direct page fetching was blocked in this session (securityboulevard.com, pipelab.org, guptadeepak.com failed with a DNS/allow-list error), so **every fact below comes from search-engine result excerpts ("snippet-only")**, not from a full read of the page. Prices are tagged:
> - **[FILED]**: purchase consideration stated in the acquirer's SEC 10-K/10-Q, seen via a search excerpt of the sec.gov filing. This is the most reliable figure.
> - **[REPORTED]**: a press estimate (Calcalist/CTech, Globes, The Information, Bloomberg, BetaNews and others). The companies did not confirm it.
> - **[ANNOUNCED]**: a price stated by the acquirer in its press release.
> - "n/v" means not verified in this session.
> Age = announcement year minus founding year (approximate).

## Q1. Deal table: every acquisition found (acquirer, target, date, price, founding, funding, employees, focus)

### Takeaway
I found about 45 relevant deals from Jan 2024 to Oct 2026, most of them from mid-2025 on. They fall into three groups:
- **Core AI-security, agent-security and NHI/agent-identity startups.** About 30 deals, with prices from about $10M to $1B and a cluster around $100–400M.
- **Large identity and data platforms.** Veza ~$1.2B, Securiti $1.73B, CyberArk $25B, Varonis $6.3B.
- **Small team/IP or acqui-hire deals.** Deepchecks, Aegis, Genie, Fabrix, Oso→Temporal, MCP Defender, SydeLabs.

Where a deal later appeared in an SEC filing, the filed consideration was usually **14–55% below the headline press figure**.

- **The niche has three price bands:**

  2. **$100–250M: product with early customers.** Entro, Stytch, CalypsoAI, Prompt, Portkey, Ryft, Permiso, LayerX, Pangea.
  3. **$300M–$1B: category leaders with about 70–120 staff and visible ARR.** Lakera, Aim, Astrix, Koi, Apono, Robust, Protect AI, SGNL, Oasis.

  A small "agent permission & audit" startup is most realistically in band 1, or in band 2 with paying design partners.
- Being young was no barrier. Several exits came under 2 years old, and even at 5–9 months (Genie, Aegis). Those mostly involved Israeli Unit-8200 teams with strong investor networks and buyers in an acquisition push (Cyera, Upwind). That pattern may not transfer to founders without those networks.

### Inferences
- **The window is not closed, but it has changed.** The fight to be the stand-alone category leader in NHI and agent identity is largely over: Astrix, Entro, Permiso, Oasis, Veza, SGNL, C1 and Apono have all been bought. Buyer demand has shifted toward cheaper capability tuck-ins and talent (Fabrix, Cyata, Deepchecks, Aegis, Genie, Oso), with AI-security deal counts rising 4x while disclosed dollar value falls. For a small "agent permission & audit" startup, this favors an early (12–30 month) exit at tens of millions over waiting to build a $300M+ company. Buyers who already own agent-identity assets (Cisco, CrowdStrike, Okta, SailPoint, PANW, ServiceNow, Workday, Cyera) are less likely to pay up again.
- **Remaining likely buyers that have not yet bought an agent-authorization or audit asset** (based only on deals found here): Microsoft, Google/Wiz, AWS, Netskope, Datadog, Snowflake, JFrog, GitLab, Atlassian, Salesforce, Ping, Saviynt, BeyondTrust, Imprivata, HashiCorp/IBM, Cloudflare, and agent-platform companies like Temporal, Anaconda or Docker. This is an inference; no reporting confirms their intent.
